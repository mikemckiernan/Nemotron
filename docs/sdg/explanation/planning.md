# Planning a Synthetic Data Generation Run

Synthetic data generation involves a sequence of decisions that determine whether the resulting data is useful for training. This page covers the questions that belong to the human — the ones that establish intent, quality criteria, and scope before any config is written or any records are generated.

The mechanics of generation (column specs, seed files, output projections) can be delegated. The answers to these questions cannot.

## What capability are you trying to produce?

Synthetic data can only teach a model to do something you can already describe. Before generating, articulate the target behavior concretely enough to design a seed file and a prompt around it.

*"Answer Greenteme Airlines customer service questions about rebooking, baggage, and refunds"* is specific enough to design around. *"Be better at customer service"* is not — it does not tell you what scenarios to seed, what persona dimensions matter, or what a correct response looks like.

If you cannot describe the target behavior concretely, generating data will not clarify it. Work backwards from a real evaluation case: what would the model need to say, in response to what kind of input, for you to conclude the capability is present?

## What does good source material look like for your domain?

The seed file and prompts are where your domain knowledge enters the pipeline. The scenarios, constraints, and vocabulary you bring to the seed scenarios shape everything the generation produces downstream. A pipeline run without domain knowledge produces generic output regardless of how well the column specs are written.

Before writing or reviewing seed material, ask:

- Are these situations representative of what real users actually encounter, including difficult and edge cases?
- Is any proprietary, private, or unlicensed content at risk of appearing in generated training data?
- Are the scenarios balanced across the dimensions that matter for the target use case — or are they skewed toward the situations that were easiest to write down?

The quality of your seed material is the strongest lever you have on the quality of what the pipeline produces.

## What makes a generated record good enough to train on?

Before scaling, be able to answer this for your domain. Not in terms of schema — whether the output parses correctly — but in terms of content. Schema correctness is verifiable automatically; content quality requires human judgment.

A useful test: if you were building a hand-curated training dataset, would you include this record? If yes, it belongs. If you would hesitate — because the response sounds evasive, the scenario is implausible, the register is wrong, or the assistant fabricated a detail — that is a signal to revise the prompt or seed before generating at scale.

Preview records answer this question cheaply. Review them yourself before delegating the judgment to metrics or automated validation. Quality issues that appear in ten preview records typically persist and compound at ten thousand.

## What are you not generating?

Synthetic data amplifies whatever signal is in the seed file and prompts. If your seed scenarios skew toward one customer segment, one channel, or one difficulty level, the model will be undertrained on the rest.

Before scaling, map the coverage your config produces against the coverage your use case requires:

- What situations, topics, or user types are over-represented?
- What situations are absent that real users will encounter?
- Is the difficulty distribution — routine, moderate, complex — appropriate for the target capability?

Identifying gaps before scaling is far cheaper than discovering them after training.

## How will you know whether it worked?

Define your evaluation before generating data, not after.

- **Keep a held-out evaluation set that is not generated by the same pipeline.** Evaluating a model trained on synthetic data using only synthetic evaluation data measures the pipeline's self-consistency, not the model's real behavior.
- **Establish a baseline before training.** Run evaluation on the unmodified model so that improvement or regression after training is measurable against a known starting point.
- **Set a threshold in advance.** A target metric defined after training is not a target — it is a rationalization. Decide before you generate what result on your evaluation would constitute success.

If you cannot define an evaluation before generating data, the data may not be ready to generate yet.

## Scope and cost

Synthetic data generation is not free. Each generated record requires one or more LLM inference calls; a large dataset at long token budgets is a meaningful API cost and a commitment of time for review and validation.

Before committing to a large generation run, confirm:

- Preview output meets the quality bar described above.
- The config, seed file, model alias, and inference parameters are captured together — if you need to reproduce the dataset or diagnose a training issue, you will need to know exactly what produced it.
- You have a plan for validating the generated records before they enter a training pipeline.

Scaling is straightforward once these conditions are met. Scaling before they are met produces a large dataset of uncertain quality.
